\title{\fb: Compiling Boolean Filters over Compressed Bitmaps into
Allocation-Free, Provably-Sized Execution Plans}

\author{William Liu}
\affiliation{%
  \institution{Exa}
  \city{San Francisco}
  \country{USA}}
\email{wliu@exa.ai}

\begin{abstract}
Roaring-format compressed bitmaps are the standard substrate for boolean
filtering in search and analytics systems, and a decade of work has driven
their per-container SIMD kernels to near the hardware ceiling. We show that
in an end-to-end filtering engine the remaining performance no longer lives
in the kernels but in the scaffolding around them: allocator traffic for
every intermediate, representation decisions re-derived inside every
operation, containers streamed that provably cannot reach the result, and
fold orders chosen blind to the memory hierarchy.

\fb{} eliminates the scaffolding by construction. A boolean filter
expression is compiled, once, into a complete execution \emph{manifest}: a
bottom-up analysis derives every operator's exact output-container set with
a \emph{proven byte ceiling per container} (so the execution path contains
no allocation or growth code at all), fixes every representation and
algorithm choice through calibrated cost models --- including a
fan-in-aware array$\to$bitmap promotion rule and a fold-order decision made
from the plan's own memory-footprint bound --- and attaches a
container-granular liveness mask so that every 64\,Ki-value block that
cannot survive the expression root is skipped before a byte of it is
streamed. The executor replays this manifest through per-thread buffer
pools and serializes results \emph{in place} inside its working arena: in
steady state a query performs zero heap allocations, and we bound its peak
working memory in closed form from the plan.

Against the standard Rust Roaring library, \fb{} wins every cell of a
2--16-way operation sweep by $1.2$--$15.5\times$ (and all but two
noise-level cells against Roaring's nightly SIMD build), evaluates a
25{,}000-tree filter corpus $3.4\times$ ($2.25\times$) faster end-to-end,
prunes selective filters up to $33\times$, and short-circuits contradictory
filters by $2{,}900\times$. \fb{} is the production filtering substrate of
Exa, a web-scale search engine, where every boolean filter predicate
evaluates through it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Boolean filtering --- ``documents matching $d \in \textit{domains} \wedge
\ell = \textit{en} \wedge t \in [t_0,t_1)$'' --- sits on the critical path
of every modern retrieval and analytics system. The dominant physical
design is by now uncontroversial: per-attribute compressed bitmaps in the
two-level Roaring layout~\cite{chambi2016,lemire2018}, whose 64\,Ki-value
\emph{containers} adaptively choose among sorted u16 arrays, 8\,KiB packed
bitmaps, and run lists, and whose per-container kernels have been the
subject of a long and effective line of SIMD
work~\cite{schlegel2011,inoue2014,lemire2016simd,han2018,fesia2020}.

Operating such a system in production at Exa led us to a diagnosis that
this paper develops into a design: \emph{the kernels are no longer the
bottleneck; the glue is}. Consider what a conventional engine does to
evaluate an $n$-ary filter tree. It allocates a fresh result structure for
every intermediate node (in our profiles of the Rust Roaring library,
roughly 6\% of end-to-end runtime is allocator time before any tail effects);
at every container it re-derives the same representation questions the
previous operator already answered; it faithfully streams, decodes, and
merges containers whose keys a one-line argument shows cannot survive the
enclosing conjunction; and it visits its operands in an order fixed by the
expression, oblivious to whether that order thrashes L1 or streams
sequentially.

\fb's design premise is that every one of those costs is a \emph{planning
failure}, not an execution necessity. Filters in serving systems are
compiled once and evaluated many times against immutable index segments;
the index is frozen by construction. Under those conditions an analysis
pass can legitimately pre-compute \emph{everything}:

\begin{itemize}
\item the exact set of output containers of every operator, each with a
  byte ceiling proven sufficient for every intermediate state its fold can
  reach (\S\ref{sec:clamps}) --- making allocation-free execution an
  \emph{invariant}, not an aspiration;
\item every algorithmic decision, as explicit cost models over quantities
  the plan already knows: kernel tier per container pair, array$\to$bitmap
  promotion as a function of per-key \emph{fan-in and} cardinality, and
  --- to our knowledge new --- the fold order of each operator, selected
  by comparing the plan's proven accumulator footprint to the cache
  (\S\ref{sec:dispatch}--\S\ref{sec:foldorder});
\item a container-granular liveness mask and compiled short-circuit
  guards, so the executor streams a provably sufficient \emph{subset} of
  the input containers --- dead blocks are never touched
  (\S\ref{sec:shortcircuit});
\item all working memory, drawn from per-thread pools and bounded in
  closed form (\S\ref{sec:memory}); results serialize \emph{in place}, so
  the fold's writes are the output's bytes.
\end{itemize}

The executor that remains is deliberately boring: a straight-line replay
of the manifest. We quantify what the boring executor is worth in
\S\ref{sec:eval}: starting from kernels at parity with the state of the
art (verified against a 310-paper survey of this literature), the
scaffolding elimination is the difference between parity and winning every
measured cell.

\textbf{Contributions.}
(1)~The plan-as-manifest architecture with proven per-container capacity
clamps and a closed-form working-memory bound
(\S\ref{sec:analysis}--\S\ref{sec:memory}).
(2)~Calibrated cost-model dispatch at three levels, including fan-in-aware
promotion and cache-footprint-driven fold-order selection, each with the
measured constants and break-even derivations
(\S\ref{sec:dispatch}--\S\ref{sec:foldorder}).
(3)~Automatic container-granular pruning across boolean expression trees,
with a soundness argument and a streamed-bytes account
(\S\ref{sec:shortcircuit}).
(4)~An evaluation with complete per-technique ablations \emph{including
negative results}, a 25{,}000-tree corpus, and a per-tree audit
methodology (\S\ref{sec:eval}); plus production deployment experience
(\S\ref{sec:deployment}).

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

We position \fb{} against four bodies of work, surveyed systematically
during its design (310 collected papers; the corpus and index accompany
the artifact).

\textbf{Compressed bitmap indexes.} The word-aligned RLE family ---
BBC~\cite{antoshenkov1995}, WAH~\cite{wu2006},
PLWAH~\cite{deliege2010}, CONCISE~\cite{colantonio2010}, and successors
--- optimizes space and bulk boolean throughput but sacrifices random
access; sorting heuristics amplify run lengths~\cite{lemire2010sorting}.
Roaring~\cite{chambi2016,lemire2016roaring,lemire2018} restructured the
field around a two-level design with per-container adaptive
representations, and is the baseline we compare against and remain
wire-compatible with. Bitmap index theory --- design spaces and encodings
--- goes back to variant indexes~\cite{oneil1997} and formal design
studies~\cite{chan1998}; bit-sliced arithmetic~\cite{rinfret2001} and
bitmapped join indices~\cite{oneil1995} are complementary encodings above
the container layer. Updatable designs (UpBit~\cite{upbit2016},
TEB~\cite{teb2020} and its in-place updates~\cite{teb2022},
CUBIT~\cite{cubit2024}) solve a problem \fb{} deliberately excludes: our
bitmaps are frozen artifacts of an indexing pipeline, and that immutability
is precisely what makes whole-plan static analysis sound. The
bitmap-versus-inverted-list boundary is mapped
empirically in~\cite{wang2017}.

\textbf{Postings compression.} The inverted-index compression literature
--- interpolative coding~\cite{moffat2000}, word-aligned
codes~\cite{anh2005}, PForDelta~\cite{zukowski2006,yan2009}, vectorized
codecs~\cite{lemire2015decoding,lemire2018svb,schlegel2010},
Elias--Fano~\cite{vigna2013,ottaviano2014,pibiri2017}, document
reordering~\cite{dhulipala2016,mackenzie2019}, surveyed
in~\cite{pibiri2021} --- optimizes a different point: sequential decode
of $d$-gapped lists. \fb{} targets the regime Roaring occupies ---
operate-in-compressed-form with random access --- but the postings
literature's lesson that \emph{layout decisions should be made by
optimization, not convention} (e.g., partitioned
Elias--Fano~\cite{ottaviano2014}) is the same philosophy \fb{} applies to
execution planning.

\textbf{Set intersection algorithms.} Adaptive and instance-optimal
intersection begins with~\cite{demaine2000} and the Barbay
line~\cite{barbay2006,barbay2010}; engineering studies for inverted
indexes include~\cite{culpepper2010,ding2011,kim2018}. The SIMD lineage
--- shuffle-based compare-all-pairs~\cite{schlegel2011,lemire2016simd},
branch-reduced merge networks~\cite{inoue2014},
QFilter~\cite{han2018}, FESIA's segment-bitmap
prefilters~\cite{fesia2020}, and VP2INTERSECT
alternatives~\cite{diez2021} --- defines today's kernel frontier;
worst-case-optimal join systems embed the same kernels at relational
scope~\cite{veldhuizen2014,aberger2016}. \fb's kernels are a portable
synthesis of this line (\S\ref{sec:dispatch}); the contribution is not a
new kernel but the compile-time selection layer above the known ones ---
closest in spirit to the query-time cost models of~\cite{kim2018}, but
static, container-granular, and coupled to capacity proofs.

\textbf{Bitmaps and signatures in systems.} Bitmap and signature machinery
pervades serving systems: classical signature
files~\cite{faloutsos1984,zobel1998}, FastBit in scientific
data~\cite{wu2009}, BitFunnel's bit-sliced signatures in the Bing search
fleet~\cite{bitfunnel2017}, bitmap filtering inside analytics engines
(Druid~\cite{druid2014,druidroaring2016}, Pinot~\cite{pinot2018},
Procella~\cite{procella2019}, ClickHouse~\cite{clickhouse2024},
Dremel~\cite{dremel2010}), social-graph retrieval~\cite{unicorn2013}, and
bit-parallel scan accelerators (BitWeaving~\cite{bitweaving2013},
ByteSlice~\cite{byteslice2015}, Column Imprints~\cite{imprints2013},
Column Sketches~\cite{sketches2018}, Data Blocks~\cite{datablocks2016}).
These papers document container structures and scan layouts; none, to our
knowledge, documents compiling boolean filter \emph{expressions} into
statically-proven execution plans. That layer --- the one this paper is
about --- appears to be tribal knowledge at best, and its absence from
the literature is corroborated by the absence of any publication for the
several industrial bitmap engines (Pilosa, Kylin-class systems) our survey
sought.

\section{Preliminaries and Notation}
\label{sec:prelim}

A \fb{} bitmap represents $S \subseteq U = [0, 2^{32})$. Key
$\kappa(x) = \lfloor x / 2^{16} \rfloor$ partitions $S$ into containers
$c_k = \{x \bmod 2^{16} : x \in S, \kappa(x) = k\}$, stored as one of:
a sorted \texttt{u16} \emph{array} ($|c| \le A_{\max} = 4096$), a packed
\emph{bitmap} of $\B = 8192$ bytes, or a \emph{run} list of $\rho(c)$
intervals ($4\rho + 2$ bytes, $\rho \le \rho_{\max} = 2047$). The stored
size of a container is
\[
\stored(c) \;=\;
\begin{cases}
2\,|c| & \text{array}\\[-1pt]
\B & \text{bitmap}\\[-1pt]
2 + 4\rho(c) & \text{runs.}
\end{cases}
\]
The on-disk format is a 16-byte header, a structure-of-arrays directory
(8 bytes per container), and 64-byte-aligned payloads; buffers are
validated once and thereafter read zero-copy, including from
\texttt{mmap}.

A \emph{filter expression} is a tree $T$ over leaves (frozen bitmaps)
with operators $\wedge, \vee, \setminus$ of arbitrary arity (binary for
$\setminus$). Same-operator edges are flattened at construction, so an
operator's \emph{fan-in} $n$ is its flattened operand count. We write $K(v)$
for the set of container keys node $v$ can emit and $\cardm_k(v)$,
$\rho_k(v)$ for per-key bounds.

\section{The Analysis Pass}
\label{sec:analysis}

Constructing an expression \emph{is} its analysis: each combinator fuses
its children's plans into a flat post-order program and computes, bottom
up, the three artifacts below. Plans are built once and replayed per
evaluation, matching the compiled-filter usage pattern of serving systems.

\subsection{Shapes}
\label{sec:shapes}

\begin{defx}[Shape]
The shape $\shape(v)$ of node $v$ is a key-sorted sequence of records
$(k, \widehat{\cardm}_k, \widehat{\rho}_k, \widehat{\tau}_k)$ giving, for
every key $v$ can emit, upper bounds on the output container's
cardinality and run count and its representation under the kernel ladder.
\end{defx}

Leaf shapes are exact (read from the directory). Interior shapes follow
the operator semantics with interval arithmetic:
$K(\wedge) = \bigcap_i K(v_i)$ with
$\widehat{\cardm}_k = \min_i \widehat{\cardm}_k(v_i)$;
$K(\vee) = \bigcup_i K(v_i)$ with
$\widehat{\cardm}_k = \sum_i \widehat{\cardm}_k(v_i)$ (saturating), and
$K(\setminus) = K(v_{\mathrm{lhs}})$ with lhs bounds. Shapes are
\emph{sound over-approximations}: $K$ contains every key the executor can
emit and every bound dominates the runtime value. Soundness is
compositional and survives flattening because each spliced child carries
its \emph{operand weight} (its flattened arity) into the parent's
analysis, so per-key fan-in bounds $\widehat{n}_k$ are also upper bounds
--- an accounting subtlety we initially got wrong (counting tree children
rather than flattened operands), caught immediately by the capacity
stress suite of \S\ref{sec:clamps}.

\subsection{Capacity clamps}
\label{sec:clamps}

From its shape, every operator receives an \emph{arena plan}: one output
slot per key of $K$, with byte capacity
\[
\capm(k) \;=\;
\begin{cases}
\max\!\big(\stored_{\mathrm{lhs}}(k),\; \sigma(\widehat{\cardm}_k)\big) & \setminus\\[2pt]
\sigma(\widehat{\cardm}_k) & \wedge\\[2pt]
\max\!\big(2\widehat{\cardm}_k,\, 2{+}4\widehat{\rho}_k,\,
  \max_i \stored_i(k)\big)\ \text{or}\ \B & \vee
\end{cases}
\]
where $\sigma(m) = 2m$ if $m \le A_{\max}$, else $\B$ (the
\emph{shrink bound}), and the union case takes $\B$ iff the plan decides
the key accumulates as a bitmap or native run form
(\S\ref{sec:dispatch}).

\begin{lemmax}[Clamp soundness]
\label{lem:clamp}
For every operator, key, and input instance consistent with the shapes,
every intermediate state of the fold at key $k$ occupies at most
$\capm(k)$ bytes.
\end{lemmax}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Case analysis on the kernel ladder. ($\wedge$) The accumulator seeds from
the per-key minimum-cardinality operand and only shrinks; if
$\widehat{\cardm}_k \le A_{\max}$ every state is an array of at most
$2\widehat{\cardm}_k$ bytes, otherwise states are runs or bitmaps, both
$\le \B = \sigma$. ($\setminus$) A key untouched by any subtrahend is a
verbatim copy ($\stored_{\mathrm{lhs}}$); a touched key only shrinks from
the lhs ($\sigma$); run-splitting states are bounded by
$2 + 4(\rho_{\mathrm{lhs}} + \textit{items}_{\mathrm{rhs}})$ bytes and
occur only under a $\B$-clamp, with conversion to bitmap forced before
$\rho$ can exceed $\rho_{\max}$. ($\vee$) Array accumulation occurs only
when the plan proved $\sum_i \widehat{\cardm}$ fits an array (every
prefix then fits); otherwise the clamp is $\B$ and all of bitmap, run
($\rho \le \rho_{\max}$), and conversion states fit. The executor never
re-derives these decisions --- \emph{the clamp is the decision} --- so
planner/kernel divergence is structurally impossible.
\end{proof}

\begin{invx}[Allocation-freeness]
The execution path contains no allocation, growth, or reallocation code.
\end{invx}

This is not an optimization applied to a fast path; the code path does
not exist. Debug builds assert every recorded container against its
clamp, and an 11{,}600-case randomized stress suite exercises the proof
obligations of Lemma~\ref{lem:clamp} (it caught, e.g., the verbatim-copy
case a $\max$ was originally missing from).

\subsection{Arena layout and the memory bound}
\label{sec:arenabound}

An operator with $n_s$ planned slots occupies one contiguous arena:
reserved output front $H(n_s) = \mathrm{align}_{64}(16 + 8n_s)$, the slot
region, an optional mirror region (\S\ref{sec:foldorder}), and fixed
scratch $2\B$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:arena}
A(P) \;=\; H(n_s) \;+\; (1{+}\delta_P)\,
\mathrm{align}_{64}\!\Big(\sum_{k \in K} \mathrm{align}_8(\capm(k))\Big)
\;+\; 2\B,
\end{equation}
with $\delta_P \in \{0,1\}$ the double-buffering bit. Since
$\capm \le \B$ always, $A(P) \le H(n_s) + (1{+}\delta_P)\, n_s \B + 2\B$;
and because $\delta_P = 1$ only when
$\sum \capm \le F_{\max} = 64\,\text{KiB}$
(\S\ref{sec:foldorder}), the mirror adds at most $64$\,KiB. For a tree,
arenas live on the evaluation stack, so peak working memory is bounded by
the plan-time quantity
$\max_{\text{stack states } s} \sum_{v \in s} A(P_v)$
--- computable at analysis time, and in practice dominated by the widest
single operator ($\le n_s\B + \text{const}$, i.e.\ $\sim$8\,KiB per
distinct output key).

\subsection{In-place serialization}
\label{sec:inplace}

Because $H(n_s)$ was reserved up front and slots were laid out with
$\capm \ge$ payload, serialization compacts payloads leftward inside the
arena and emits the arena's own buffer as the result.

\begin{lemmax}[Leftward compaction]
\label{lem:inplace}
Writing outputs in key order at
$d_i = \mathrm{base} + \sum_{j<i} \mathrm{size}_j$ (with per-type
alignment) never overwrites an unread source: $d_i \le s_i$ for all $i$.
\end{lemmax}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Sources sit at $s_i = H + \sum_{j<i}\mathrm{align}_8(\capm_j) + \Delta_i
\ge \mathrm{base} + \sum_{j<i}\mathrm{size}_j = d_i$, since
$\mathrm{base} \le H$ and $\mathrm{size}_j \le \capm_j$ pointwise;
mirror-side sources lie beyond the entire primary region, strengthening
the inequality. Alignment rounding preserves it because sources are at
least as aligned as destinations.
\end{proof}

The practical content of Lemma~\ref{lem:inplace} is a whole pass of
memory traffic \emph{removed}: a conventional engine copies every output
byte from working memory into a result allocation. Profiling located this
precisely --- \fb's 16-way difference fold already beat Roaring
($185$ vs.\ $190\us$) while the end-to-end operation lost ($206$ vs.\
$190\us$); the entire deficit was the output copy. Eliminating it turned
every large-result cell around (\S\ref{sec:eval:abl}).

\section{Cost-Model Dispatch}
\label{sec:dispatch}

Execution decisions are functions of plan-known quantities, with
constants calibrated once per microarchitecture
(Table~\ref{tab:constants}) and every crossover ablated
(\S\ref{sec:eval:abl}).

\begin{table}[t]
\caption{Calibrated kernel constants (Apple M-series, medians).}
\label{tab:constants}
\small
\begin{tabular}{llr}
\toprule
$c_b$ & 8-lane merge block (compare-all-pairs) & $1.45\nsu$ \\
$c_m$ & per element streamed through an array merge & $0.85\nsu$ \\
$c_s$ & per value scattered into a bitmap & $1.2\nsu$ \\
$c_0$ & bitmap clear + popcount (fixed) & $\approx450\nsu$ \\
$c_p$ & bitmap membership probe (hoisted loop) & $\approx1\nsu$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\textbf{Kernel tiers.} For an array pair $|A| \le |B|$:
balanced pairs ($|B| \le 4|A|$) run a shuffle-merge at
$c_b\,\lceil(|A|{+}|B|)/8\rceil$ --- compare-all-pairs over rotations of
\emph{both} operands (5 permutations per block rather than 7) with
branchless table compaction; heavy skew runs galloping binary search at
$\approx c_g |A| \log_2 |B|$, taken only under the margin test
$8\,|A| \log_2 |B| < |B|$, i.e.\ only when it beats the middle tier's
linear window advance by $8\times$ --- binary search mispredicts, so
paper parity is insufficient; the middle tier is a galloping
broadcast-scan. Every tier is reached by data observed in production
shapes, and the two boundaries were set empirically (the cost-model gallop
gate replaced a fixed ratio that regressed five-way conjunctions by
26\%).

\textbf{Fan-in-aware promotion.} A union key accumulating as an array
costs each merge a re-stream of the accumulator; with fan-in $n$ and
summed cardinality $s$ (even splits),
\begin{equation}
C_{\mathrm{arr}}(n,s) \approx c_m\, s\!\left(\tfrac{n+1}{2} -
\tfrac1n\right), \qquad
C_{\mathrm{bmp}}(s) \approx c_0 + c_s\, s .
\end{equation}
Solving $C_{\mathrm{arr}} > C_{\mathrm{bmp}}$ with
Table~\ref{tab:constants} yields no crossover at $n \le 3$ for any
$s \le A_{\max}$, and thresholds $s \gtrsim 980, 480, 200$ at
$n = 4, 5, 8$; we encode it as
$\textsc{promote}(n,s) \equiv n \ge 4 \wedge s\,(n-3) > 1000$.
The classical fixed threshold (promote at $s > 256$ regardless of $n$,
as in CRoaring-derived engines) is dominated: tuned low it converts the
OR-groups of low-fan-in conjunctions into bitmaps their parent consumes
more slowly (measured $+29\%$ on that shape); tuned high it strands wide
unions in array form (measured $3.7\times$ on 4-way unions,
Table~\ref{tab:abl}). Fan-in bounds come from the weighted shapes of
\S\ref{sec:shapes}; the clamp is the single authority at execution.

\section{Fold Order and the Memory Hierarchy}
\label{sec:foldorder}

An $n$-ary fold traverses keys $\times$ operands. \emph{Key-major} order
completes each key before the next: one accumulator stays register/L1
resident, but each of the $n$ operand streams is resumed once per key ---
$nK$ stream resumptions across $K$ keys, each restarting the prefetcher.
\emph{Partner-major} order streams operand $j$ end-to-end against all
accumulators before operand $j{+}1$: perfectly sequential input traffic,
but the entire accumulator set is revisited $n$ times.

Both orders move the same bytes; they differ in \emph{arrangement}, and
each is optimal in a regime the plan can identify. Profiling 16-way
differences located a $+50\%$ per-block anomaly entirely in memory: the
merge kernel ran at $1.45\nsu$/block standalone and $1.56\nsu$ in a
single-key control, but $2.17\nsu$ inside the 32-key key-major fold ---
interleaved resumption of 16 streams defeating L1 --- while the identical
workload under partner-major recovered the standalone rate. Dense unions
then exhibited the mirror image: with per-key bitmap accumulators the
accumulator set is $K \cdot \B$ (128\,KiB at $K{=}16$), revisiting it
each pass thrashes L1, and key-major won by 12\%.

The plan already holds the discriminating quantity: the accumulator
footprint $F(P) = \sum_k \capm(k)$ of Eq.~\ref{eq:arena}. \fb{} folds
partner-major iff $F(P) \le F_{\max} = 64\,\text{KiB}$ (half of L1D,
leaving room for one streaming operand and the mirror side), allocating
the mirror region ($\delta_P{=}1$) so array merges alternate slot sides
instead of staging through scratch. Intersection is the measured
exception: a key-count scaling control (16-way AND, 1 vs.\ 32 keys:
$695\nsu \to 21.6\us$, $31.1\times$ for $32\times$ keys) shows no
super-linear term --- the accumulator collapses after the first fold and
partners are galloped into, not re-streamed --- and partner-major seeding
would require clamping by the \emph{driver's} cardinality rather than the
per-key minimum, weakening Lemma~\ref{lem:clamp}. Each operator thus runs
the order measurement assigned it: difference and union dispatch on
$F(P)$; intersection stays key-major, by evidence rather than default.

\section{Streaming the Provable Minimum}
\label{sec:shortcircuit}

The manifest eliminates work at three granularities. Throughout,
``container granularity'' is Roaring's own: 64\,Ki-value blocks.

\subsection{Hole-punching}
\label{sec:holepunch}

\begin{defx}[Liveness mask]
$\mask(T) = K(\mathrm{root}(T))$ under the shape recurrences:
intersection of child key sets at $\wedge$, union at $\vee$, left child
at $\setminus$.
\end{defx}

\begin{lemmax}[Pruning soundness]
\label{lem:mask}
For any leaf $\ell$ and key $k \notin \mask(T)$, removing container
$c_k(\ell)$ from the input changes no output value. Pruning is monotone:
$\mask$ over-approximates the true surviving key set, so no contributing
container is ever dropped.
\end{lemmax}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By induction on $T$: a key absent from $K(v)$ yields the empty container
at $v$ under all three operators, and empty containers are identities
(for $\vee$) or annihilators (for $\wedge$; left-absorbing for
$\setminus$) that propagate to the root. Since the executor's keys are a
subset of shape keys (soundness, \S\ref{sec:shapes}), masking to
$\mask(T)$ preserves every output.
\end{proof}

The planner derives $\mask$ automatically whenever
$|K(\mathrm{root})| < \max_i |K(v_i)|$ --- i.e., exactly when pruning is
guaranteed non-trivial --- materializes it as an 8\,KiB bitset, and every
leaf cursor skips dead keys in place.

\textbf{Streamed-bytes account.} Without the mask, evaluating $T$ streams
$\sum_{\ell} \sum_{k \in K(\ell)} \stored(c_k(\ell))$ input bytes; with
it, keys outside $\mask(T)$ contribute zero:
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\text{stream}} \;=\; \sum_{\ell \in \mathrm{leaves}(T)}\;
\sum_{k \in K(\ell) \setminus \mask(T)} \stored\!\big(c_k(\ell)\big).
\end{equation}
In the selective-filter benchmark (one 4-key conjunct against four
256-key disjunction legs) this removes $\sim\!98\%$ of input bytes and
yields $7.35\us$ vs.\ $241\us$ unpruned ($33\times$) and $374\us$ for
Roaring's SIMD build; across the 25k-tree corpus, enabling automatic
derivation was worth $-15.3\%$ end-to-end ($p<0.05$). We believe this is
the first published container-granular analogue of zone-map pruning that
acts \emph{across} a boolean expression tree rather than along a scan.

\subsection{Compiled short-circuits}

The program carries \emph{guard} instructions after every non-flattened
$\wedge$-operand and every $\setminus$-minuend: if the operand just
produced is empty, the guard pops the partial operands and jumps past the
fold --- sibling subtrees are never evaluated. Guards encode
\emph{relative} jump offsets so they survive splicing into enclosing
plans. A contradictory filter (empty subtree beside an expensive
disjunction) evaluates in $133\nsu$ against $392\us$ for Roaring
($\sim\!2900\times$): the disjunction is simply never run. Within
operators, intersection fuses population count into the AND and abandons
a key the moment it empties; partners fold most-selective-first;
partner-major passes skip dead slots.

\section{Memory Discipline}
\label{sec:memory}

Four per-thread pools back execution: the op arena
(Eq.~\ref{eq:arena}), fold cursor/reference scratch, the evaluator's
operand stack, and the result buffers themselves. Pools hand out
lifetime-erased buffers that are only ever stored empty; arenas chain as
operator inputs without serialization until the root; and in-place
serialization (Lemma~\ref{lem:inplace}) swaps the arena's buffer out as
the result while a pooled result buffer rotates back --- the pools
circulate, so \emph{steady-state evaluation performs zero heap
allocations}, a property we verify by sampling profiles (the allocator
does not appear) rather than assert. First use on a thread allocates
once and joins the cycle; there is no pre-warm API to misuse.

What this removes, quantitatively: the baseline's allocator time
($\approx\!6\%$ of Roaring's profile in our workloads --- a
fresh container vector per intermediate operation); the output copy
(\S\ref{sec:inplace}), worth $2$--$9\us$ on sparse 8--16-way operations
and up to $30\%$ on dense 2-way ones; and the allocator-induced tail
variance that motivated the design in production
(\S\ref{sec:deployment}).

\subsection{Ablations, including the failures}
\label{sec:eval:abl}

Every mechanism entered under a keep-only-if-faster rule
(Table~\ref{tab:abl}); the rejected designs bound the space as sharply as
the accepted ones. Three deserve emphasis. (i)~The in-place-serialization
ablation is a case study in profiling the right layer: the fold beat the
baseline while the operation lost, and the gap was exactly the output
copy. (ii)~The branch study: an adversarial-vs-predictable input sweep
showed the merge advance branches cost \emph{zero} (predictable-pattern
$1.43\us$ vs.\ adversarial $1.45\us$ at identical work), a branchless
rewrite of the scalar union merge \emph{regressed} $26\%$, yet branchless
table compaction of the intersection emit won $2.2\times$ at dense
overlap --- branchless transformations pay only where decisions are
dense \emph{and} data-dependent, and each direction is worth measuring.
(iii)~The per-tree audit loop --- time all 25k trees on both engines,
explain the worst offender structurally, fix the class --- drove two
mechanisms into the system (the hoisted bitmap probe, automatic
hole-punching) and is, we would argue, a transferable methodology.

\subsection{Positioning against the kernel literature}

Because \fb's claims live above the kernels, we verified the kernels
themselves are not the story: our balanced merge matches the
compare-all-pairs family~\cite{schlegel2011,lemire2016simd} with the
both-operand rotation of~\cite{diez2021}; our union network is
Inoue-style~\cite{inoue2014}; our skew tiers subsume the galloping
tradition~\cite{demaine2000,culpepper2010}. A per-block throughput
comparison against the Rust Roaring SIMD kernels shows parity to
$+16\%$ in \fb's favor at equal work --- consistent with our thesis that
the end-to-end gaps of Tables~\ref{tab:ops}--\ref{tab:trees} are
scaffolding, not kernels.

\section{Deployment at Exa}
\label{sec:deployment}

\fb{} is the foundational bitmap structure for \emph{all} boolean
filtering at Exa, a production web-scale search engine: every filter
predicate --- domain, language, time, category, and arbitrary boolean
combinations --- compiles to a \fb{} expression evaluated against frozen
per-segment bitmaps served from memory maps. Three properties carry the
operational weight. Plans compile once per query and replay across
segments, so analysis cost amortizes exactly as \S\ref{sec:analysis}
assumes. The zero-allocation invariant removes the allocator from tail
latency entirely --- the original production complaint that motivated the
design. And hole-punching converts the dominant production pattern (one
narrow conjunct beside wide disjunctions) from the engine's worst case
into its best. The frozen-segment model is not a restriction we tolerate
but the assumption that makes the whole analysis sound; updates arrive as
new segments from the indexing pipeline, as in any LSM-style search
architecture.

\section{Conclusion}

After a decade of kernel optimization, the residual
$1.2$--$15\times$ in boolean bitmap filtering was hiding in the
scaffolding. \fb{} reclaims it with an analysis pass strong enough to
make execution trivial: every output pre-sized under a proven clamp,
every algorithm and fold order chosen by a calibrated model over
plan-known quantities, every dead container pruned before it is
streamed, and every byte of working memory pooled and bounded in closed
form --- so the executor just replays a proof. The library, differential
suites, 25k-tree corpus, audit tooling, and the 310-paper survey index
are open source.
