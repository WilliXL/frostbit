\title{\fb: Proof-Carrying Execution Plans for Boolean Filtering over
Compressed Bitmaps}

\author{William Liu}
\affiliation{%

  \city{San Francisco}
  \country{USA}}
\email{wliu@exa.ai}

\begin{abstract}
Boolean filtering over Roaring-format compressed bitmaps is a core
primitive of search and analytics systems. Prior work optimizes such
queries dynamically: cost models select per-node algorithms and
representations at evaluation time, buffers are allocated as results
materialize, and pruning decisions are made while operands are consumed.
This paper develops the static alternative for the read-only setting in
which serving systems actually operate --- immutable index segments and
filters compiled once, evaluated many times. \fb{} compiles a boolean
expression into a \emph{proof-carrying execution plan}: a bottom-up
analysis derives every operator's output-container set together with a
byte ceiling per container that is sound for every intermediate state of
the fold, from which three guarantees follow at plan time rather than
run time --- execution is allocation-free by construction with a
closed-form working-memory bound; a container-granular liveness mask
prunes, before any input byte is read, every 64\,Ki-value block that
cannot contribute to the result of the whole expression; and each
operator's fold order is selected by comparing its proven accumulator
footprint to the cache hierarchy. Representation and kernel choices are
resolved by cost models over plan-derived quantities, including a
fan-in-aware array-to-bitmap promotion rule that dominates, under the
calibrated model, the fixed cardinality thresholds used in deployed
Roaring implementations.

Against both configurations of the standard Rust Roaring library, with
all $n$-ary baseline calls routed through the library's documented
\texttt{MultiOps} fast path, \fb{} wins 34 of a 2--16-way operation
sweep's 36 cells against both builds: every sorted-array cell
($1.25$--$12.2\times$), every packed-bitmap cell but a
binary-difference tie, and every run-container cell but 16-way
intersection ($0.87\times$, the single loss). End-to-end it evaluates
a 25{,}000-tree filter corpus $2.7\times$ and $2.2\times$ faster, with
per-mechanism ablations --- including rejected designs --- isolating
the contribution of each planning decision. \fb{} is deployed as the
filtering substrate of a commercial web-scale search engine.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Serving systems evaluate boolean filter predicates --- conjunctions,
disjunctions, and negations over per-attribute document sets --- on the
critical path of every query. The dominant physical representation is the
Roaring bitmap~\cite{chambi2016,lemire2018}: a two-level structure whose
64\,Ki-value \emph{containers} adaptively select among sorted arrays,
packed bitmaps, and run encodings, and whose per-container kernels have
been extensively optimized with SIMD
techniques~\cite{schlegel2011,inoue2014,lemire2016simd,han2018,fesia2020}.

Optimizing the \emph{expression} above the containers is a problem with a
substantial history. Johnson~\cite{johnson1999} measured the large
algorithm-choice space for compressed-bitmap operations; Amer-Yahia and
Johnson~\cite{ameryahia2000} built a dynamic-programming optimizer that
selects per-node algorithms and intermediate formats for boolean
expression trees over RLE-compressed bitmaps; Kaser and
Lemire~\cite{kaser2016} fitted cost formulas to choose evaluation
strategies for multi-operand queries over word-aligned (EWAH)
bitmaps; Kim et al.~\cite{kim2018} develop calibrated cost models for
per-query intersection-algorithm selection in web search; and a
theoretical line establishes worst-case-optimal evaluation of
union-intersection expressions~\cite{chiniforooshan2005,bille2007}.
These optimizers decide either immediately before each evaluation,
from estimated operand statistics~\cite{ameryahia2000,kaser2016,
kim2018}, or during it, adapting to observed
selectivities~\cite{raman2007}; in both cases the execution machinery
beneath them allocates and sizes intermediate results as they
materialize, and none emits guarantees about the execution it has
chosen.

This paper examines the opposite point in the design space, motivated by
the regime in which filtering actually runs in production: index
segments are immutable artifacts of an indexing pipeline, so every
statistic a plan depends on --- each leaf's exact per-container key
set, cardinality, and representation --- is exact, not estimated, and
can never be invalidated. In this setting adaptivity has nothing to
adapt to, and we show that the entire optimization surface can be
discharged \emph{statically} at plan construction (whose cost is
included in every measurement we report), yielding plans that carry
guarantees no estimate-driven or adaptive optimizer provides:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Allocation-freeness as a plan-time invariant.} The analysis
  derives, for every operator, its exact output-container key set and a
  per-container byte ceiling (\emph{capacity clamp}) sound for every
  intermediate state of the fold (\S\ref{sec:clamps}). Consequently the
  execution path contains no allocation or growth code, working memory is
  bounded in closed form (\S\ref{sec:arenabound}), and results serialize
  in place inside the working arena (\S\ref{sec:inplace}). Engines such
  as Trill~\cite{trill2015} and Photon~\cite{photon2022} pool and bound
  allocations operationally; CRoaring pre-sizes individual operations by
  cardinality analysis~\cite{chambi2016}. \fb{} differs in deriving the
  bound for the \emph{whole expression} from the plan, and in the
  stronger consequence that the steady-state allocation count is exactly
  zero, a property we verify empirically.
\item \textbf{Static, expression-wide, container-granular pruning.} An
  upward pass computes each node's surviving key set; when the root's set
  is narrower than a child's, the plan carries a liveness mask and every
  leaf cursor skips dead containers before they are read
  (\S\ref{sec:holepunch}). Zone maps prune scans against per-block
  metadata~\cite{moerkotte1998,snowflake2016}; skip pointers and
  block-max structures prune \emph{dynamically} during list
  traversal~\cite{moffat1996,ding2011}; the Java Roaring library's
  \texttt{workShyAnd} computes a key mask at run time for flat $n$-ary
  intersection~\cite{roaringjava}; partition-level prefilters prune
  pairwise~\cite{fesia2020,hero2024}. \fb{} generalizes these to a
  \emph{static} mask over arbitrary
  AND/OR/DIFF trees, derived from the plan itself, with a soundness
  argument (Lemma~\ref{lem:mask}) and with no per-evaluation decision
  cost.
\item \textbf{Fold order selected from a proven footprint.} $n$-ary folds
  admit two orders --- complete each key across all operands, or stream
  each operand across all keys --- which are the container-algebra
  analogues of document-at-a-time and term-at-a-time
  evaluation~\cite{turtle1995}, and which we show are memory-optimal in
  disjoint regimes on modern hierarchies. Cache-conscious cost models for
  operator layout are classical~\cite{wolf1991,manegold2002,boncz2005};
  \fb's distinction is that the discriminating quantity --- the
  accumulator footprint --- is not an estimate but the sum of proven
  clamps, so the decision inherits the plan's soundness
  (\S\ref{sec:foldorder}).
\end{itemize}

Beneath the plan, representation and kernel selection are governed by
explicit cost models over plan-derived quantities
(\S\ref{sec:dispatch}); the kernels themselves synthesize the published
state of the art~\cite{schlegel2011,inoue2014,lemire2016simd,diez2021}
and are measured at parity with the baseline's, so that end-to-end
differences are attributable primarily to the planning layer rather
than kernel quality (\S\ref{sec:eval}).

\textbf{Contributions.}
\textbf{C1.} A compilation scheme from boolean container-algebra
expressions to proof-carrying plans\footnote{The term is borrowed from
proof-carrying code~\cite{necula1997} and is used in the same spirit as
proof-carrying plans for AI planning~\cite{schwaab2019}: the artifact
transports obligations discharged before execution. \fb's plans carry
compiler-derived bounds enforced by construction and checked by
assertion and stress testing, not independently verifiable
certificates.}: sound output shapes, per-container
capacity clamps with a case-analytic soundness argument, and a
closed-form working-memory bound; allocation-free execution and in-place
result serialization follow as invariants
(\S\ref{sec:analysis},~\S\ref{sec:memory}).
\textbf{C2.} A static, container-granular liveness analysis for arbitrary
AND/OR/DIFF trees with a soundness proof and a streamed-bytes account,
automatically enabled when the plan proves it profitable
(\S\ref{sec:holepunch}).
\textbf{C3.} Plan-time algorithm selection with derived break-evens: a
fan-in-aware array-to-bitmap promotion rule that dominates fixed
cardinality thresholds, and footprint-gated fold-order selection,
including the control experiments that scoped where each applies
(\S\ref{sec:dispatch},~\S\ref{sec:foldorder}).
\textbf{C4.} An evaluation against both configurations of the Rust
Roaring library with complete per-mechanism ablations including negative
results, a 25{,}000-tree corpus, and a per-tree audit methodology;
\fb{} is deployed in production and released as open source
(\S\ref{sec:eval},~\S\ref{sec:deployment}).

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Expression-level optimization of bitmap and set queries.}
The closest line to this work begins with performance studies of
compressed-bitmap operation choices~\cite{johnson1999} and the
Amer-Yahia--Johnson optimizer~\cite{ameryahia2000}, which plans boolean
expression trees over RLE bitmaps by dynamic programming over
per-node algorithm and format choices under density-based cost models.
Kaser and Lemire~\cite{kaser2016} extend cost-based strategy selection
to multi-operand queries over EWAH-compressed bitmaps (including
heuristics for intermediate materialization), and Kim et
al.~\cite{kim2018} build and validate calibrated cost models that pick
intersection algorithms per query for web-search workloads.
Adaptive execution takes the dynamic commitment further: Raman et
al.~\cite{raman2007} interleave RID-list intersection strategies based
on observed selectivities precisely because static plans mispredict.
The theory community established worst-case-optimal evaluation of
union-intersection expressions~\cite{chiniforooshan2005,bille2007}, and
semijoin-style liveness reasoning over query plans dates to
Yannakakis~\cite{yannakakis1981}, recently revived as predicate
transfer~\cite{predtransfer2024}. \fb{} occupies a point none of these
target. Like the pre-execution optimizers it plans before evaluating,
but from the \emph{exact} per-container metadata of frozen operands
rather than estimated statistics, and its output is stronger than an
algorithm selection: the plan carries discharged obligations ---
capacity clamps, allocation-freeness, a closed-form memory bound,
liveness masks --- that estimate-driven planners cannot emit (their
decisions may be wrong and must remain revisable) and that adaptive
executors by construction do not have before execution. The trade is
explicit: \fb{} forgoes runtime adaptivity on interior results
(\S\ref{sec:limitations}) in exchange for guarantees and an executor
containing no decision code.

\textbf{Compressed bitmap structures.} The word-aligned RLE family
(BBC~\cite{antoshenkov1995}, WAH~\cite{wu2006}, PLWAH~\cite{deliege2010},
CONCISE~\cite{colantonio2010}) optimizes space and bulk boolean
throughput; sorting amplifies run lengths~\cite{lemire2010sorting}.
Roaring~\cite{chambi2016,lemire2016roaring,lemire2018} introduced the
two-level container design \fb{} adopts; \fb's frozen format transcodes
losslessly to and from portable Roaring serialization at container
granularity. Index-level designs --- variant indexes~\cite{oneil1997}, bitmap
join indices~\cite{oneil1995}, bit-sliced arithmetic~\cite{rinfret2001},
design-space studies~\cite{chan1998} --- and updatable structures
(UpBit~\cite{upbit2016}, TEB~\cite{teb2020,teb2022},
CUBIT~\cite{cubit2024}) are complementary: \fb{} deliberately targets
immutable segments, which is the assumption that makes its static
analysis sound. The bitmap-versus-inverted-list boundary is mapped
in~\cite{wang2017}.

\textbf{Container kernels.} Adaptive and instance-optimal set
intersection descends from Demaine et al.~\cite{demaine2000} and the
Barbay line~\cite{barbay2006,barbay2010}, with engineering treatments
for inverted indexes~\cite{culpepper2010,ding2011konig}. The SIMD
lineage comprises shuffle-based
compare-all-pairs~\cite{schlegel2011,lemire2016simd}, branch-reduced
merge networks~\cite{inoue2014}, QFilter~\cite{han2018}, FESIA's
segment prefilters~\cite{fesia2020}, hierarchical partitioning
(HERO~\cite{hero2024}), and VP2INTERSECT
alternatives~\cite{diez2021}; worst-case-optimal join systems embed the
same kernels relationally~\cite{veldhuizen2014,aberger2016}. \fb's
kernels synthesize this line; CRoaring additionally ships per-pair
dispatch heuristics (galloping thresholds, cardinality-based
preallocation, deferred aggregation)~\cite{chambi2016,lemire2018} that
the Rust port lacks, a distinction our evaluation accounts for
(\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\textbf{Block skipping and scan pruning.} Zone maps and small
materialized aggregates prune blocks against predicate
metadata~\cite{moerkotte1998,snowflake2016}; self-indexing inverted
files skip within lists~\cite{moffat1996}; block-max indexes prune
score-bounded blocks dynamically~\cite{ding2011}. These techniques
prune either along a single scan or during traversal. \fb's liveness
analysis differs in operating \emph{across} an arbitrary boolean
expression statically, at Roaring's own container granularity, before
any operand byte is read.

\textbf{Memory discipline in query engines.} Streaming and vectorized
engines bound steady-state allocation
operationally~\cite{trill2015,photon2022,boncz2005}; cache-aware
cost modeling of operator behavior is
classical~\cite{wolf1991,manegold2002}; plan-time memory-requirement
characterization has lineage in continuous queries over
streams~\cite{arasu2002}; and representation selection for filter
evaluation has a recent vectorized-execution
treatment~\cite{ngom2021}. \fb's contribution here is the
coupling: the memory bound is an artifact of the same analysis that
proves capacity soundness, and serialization reuses the working arena
(\S\ref{sec:inplace}), which we have not seen documented for bitmap
query execution.

\textbf{Bitmaps in deployed systems.} Signature and bitmap machinery
appears throughout serving systems --- signature
files~\cite{faloutsos1984,zobel1998}, FastBit~\cite{wu2009},
BitFunnel~\cite{bitfunnel2017}, analytics engines~\cite{druid2014,
druidroaring2016,pinot2018,procella2019,clickhouse2024,dremel2010},
graph search~\cite{unicorn2013}, and bit-parallel scan
accelerators~\cite{bitweaving2013,byteslice2015,imprints2013,
sketches2018,datablocks2016}. These document container structures and
scan layouts; the expression-compilation layer described here is not, to
our knowledge, described in any of them.

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}

A \fb{} bitmap represents $S \subseteq [0, 2^{32})$, partitioned by key
$\kappa(x) = \lfloor x/2^{16} \rfloor$ into containers over
$[0, 2^{16})$, each stored as a sorted \texttt{u16} array
($|c| \le A_{\max} = 4096$), a packed bitmap of $\B = 8192$ bytes, or a
run list of $\rho(c) \le \rho_{\max} = 2047$ intervals:
\[
\stored(c) =
\begin{cases}
2|c| & \text{array},\\
\B & \text{bitmap},\\
2+4\rho(c) & \text{runs.}
\end{cases}
\]
The serialized form is a 16-byte header, a structure-of-arrays directory
(8 bytes per container), and 64-byte-aligned payloads; buffers are
validated once at the trust boundary and read zero-copy thereafter,
including from memory maps.

A filter expression is a tree over frozen-bitmap leaves with operators
$\wedge$, $\vee$ (arbitrary arity), and $\setminus$ (binary). Bare
complement is not an operator; filter languages reach the engine with
negation normalized into difference against an enclosing operand or an
explicit universe bitmap. Same-operator edges are flattened at
construction; an operator's \emph{fan-in} $n$ is its flattened operand
count. $K(v)$ denotes the key set node $v$ can emit; hats
($\widehat{\cdot}$) denote plan-time upper bounds.

\section{Plan Compilation}
\label{sec:analysis}

Expression construction and analysis are fused: each combinator splices
its children's programs into a flat post-order instruction sequence and
computes the artifacts below bottom-up. A plan is specific to its
operand set --- leaf shapes are exact properties of particular frozen
bitmaps --- and may be evaluated against them arbitrarily many times;
construction is cheap enough to sit on the query path and is included
in every end-to-end measurement in \S\ref{sec:eval}.

\subsection{Shapes}
\label{sec:shapes}

\begin{defx}[Shape]
The shape of node $v$ is the key-sorted sequence
$\{(k, \widehat{\mathrm{card}}_k, \widehat{\rho}_k, \widehat{\tau}_k) :
k \in K(v)\}$ of per-key bounds on output cardinality, run count, and
representation under the kernel decision ladder.
\end{defx}

Leaf shapes are exact. Interior shapes follow operator semantics with
saturating interval arithmetic ($\cap$/$\min$ for $\wedge$;
$\cup$/$\Sigma$ for $\vee$; left projection for $\setminus$) and are
sound over-approximations of any execution. Because flattening changes
arity, each spliced child contributes its \emph{operand weight} to the
parent's per-key fan-in bound $\widehat{n}_k$; weights make
$\widehat{n}_k$ an exact upper bound on executor fan-in, which the
promotion rule of \S\ref{sec:dispatch} requires. (An early
implementation that bounded fan-in by tree arity rather than flattened
weight produced plans inconsistent with their kernels; the capacity
stress suite of \S\ref{sec:clamps} detects this class of error, and the
final design eliminates it by making the plan's clamp the sole authority
for representation choice.)

\subsection{Capacity clamps}
\label{sec:clamps}

Each operator receives an arena plan: one slot per key of its shape,
with capacity
\[
\capm(k) =
\begin{cases}
\sigma(\widehat{\mathrm{card}}_k) & \wedge\\[2pt]
\max\big(\stored_{\mathrm{lhs}}(k),\, \sigma(\widehat{\mathrm{card}}_k)\big) & \setminus\\[2pt]
\max\big(2\widehat{\mathrm{card}}_k,\, 2{+}4\widehat{\rho}_k,\,
\max_i \stored_i(k)\big) \text{ or } \B & \vee
\end{cases}
\]
where $\sigma(m) = 2m$ for $m \le A_{\max}$ and $\B$ otherwise, and the
union clamp is $\B$ exactly when the plan assigns the key a bitmap or
native-run accumulator (\S\ref{sec:dispatch}). One escape hatch trades
slack for analysis cost: for tiny $\wedge$/$\setminus$ one-shots
($\le$16\,KiB of input, $\le$32 driving keys), where \S\ref{sec:eval}
shows the analysis walk dominating, the planner substitutes the
trivially sound clamp $\capm(k) = \B$ over the driving input's keys
and skips the capacity walk entirely.

\begin{propx}[Capacity soundness]
\label{prop:clamp}
For every operator, key, and input consistent with the shapes, every
intermediate state of the fold at key $k$ occupies at most $\capm(k)$
bytes.
\end{propx}

\begin{proof}[Argument]
By case analysis on the accumulator ladder. For $\wedge$, the
accumulator seeds from the per-key minimum-cardinality operand and only
shrinks. One executor invariant carries the run case: a run encoding
($2{+}4\rho$ bytes) can exceed its array encoding when $\rho$
approaches the cardinality, so run-form accumulators are permitted only
inside $\B$-clamped slots, where any $\rho \le \rho_{\max}$ container
fits; under an array clamp the executor expands a run seed to array
form at seeding. Every reachable state then fits
$\sigma(\widehat{\mathrm{card}}_k)$. For $\setminus$, untouched keys are
verbatim copies; touched keys shrink from the left operand, and
run-splitting states, bounded by
$2 + 4(\rho_{\mathrm{lhs}} + \textit{items}_{\mathrm{rhs}})$ bytes,
arise only under a $\B$ clamp with conversion to bitmap forced before
$\rho_{\max}$ is exceeded. For $\vee$, array accumulation is planned
only when the summed bound fits an array, in which case every prefix
fits; otherwise the clamp is $\B$, which dominates bitmap, run, and
conversion states. The executor never re-derives these decisions ---
the clamp \emph{is} the decision --- so plan/kernel divergence cannot
occur.
\end{proof}

We state Proposition~\ref{prop:clamp} and its relatives formally because
the memory bound and the executor's structure depend on them, not as
contributions to theory: the arguments are elementary. Their value is
operational --- debug builds assert every recorded container against
its clamp, and an 11{,}600-case randomized stress suite exercises the
obligations.

\begin{invx}[Allocation-freeness]
\label{inv:alloc}
The execution path contains no allocation, growth, or reallocation
code.
\end{invx}

\subsection{Working-memory bound}
\label{sec:arenabound}

An operator with $n_s$ slots occupies one arena comprising a reserved
directory region $H(n_s) = \mathrm{align}_{64}(16 + 8n_s)$, the slot
region, an optional mirror region for side-flipped folds
(\S\ref{sec:foldorder}), and fixed scratch $2\B$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:arena}
A(P) = H(n_s) + (1{+}\delta_P)\,
\mathrm{align}_{64}\!\Big(\textstyle\sum_{k}
\mathrm{align}_8(\capm(k))\Big) + 2\B .
\end{equation}
Since $\capm \le \B$, $A(P) \le H(n_s) + (1{+}\delta_P)n_s\B + 2\B$, and
$\delta_P = 1$ only when $\sum_k \capm(k) \le F_{\max} = 64$\,KiB, so
the mirror contributes at most 64\,KiB. For a tree, arenas are live
along the evaluation stack, and peak working memory is the plan-time
quantity $\max_{s} \sum_{v \in s} A(P_v)$ over stack states $s$.

\subsection{In-place serialization}
\label{sec:inplace}

Because the directory region is reserved and slot capacities dominate
payloads, serialization compacts payloads leftward within the arena and
emits the arena's own buffer as the result.

\begin{propx}[Leftward compaction]
\label{prop:inplace}
Writing outputs in key order at
$d_i = \mathrm{base} + \sum_{j<i}\mathrm{size}_j$ (with per-type
alignment) satisfies $d_i \le s_i$ for every source offset $s_i$; no
unread byte is overwritten.
\end{propx}

\begin{proof}[Argument]
$s_i = H + \sum_{j<i}\mathrm{align}_8(\capm_j) + \Delta_i \ge
\mathrm{base} + \sum_{j<i}\mathrm{size}_j = d_i$, as
$\mathrm{base} \le H$ and $\mathrm{size}_j \le \capm_j$ pointwise;
mirror-side sources lie beyond the primary region, and alignment
rounding preserves the inequality because sources are at least as
aligned as destinations.
\end{proof}

The operational content is one full pass of memory traffic removed
relative to engines that copy results out of working memory.
Profiling during development isolated this term directly: \fb's 16-way
difference fold was already faster than the baseline ($185$ vs.\
$190\us$) while the end-to-end operation was slower ($206$ vs.\
$190\us$); the gap was exactly the output copy, and eliminating it
recovered the fold's advantage end-to-end (\S\ref{sec:eval:abl}).

\section{Representation and Kernel Selection}
\label{sec:dispatch}

Execution decisions are functions of plan-derived quantities with
constants calibrated per microarchitecture (Table~\ref{tab:constants}).

\textbf{Sorted-array kernel tiers.} For an array pair with
$|A| \le |B|$: balanced pairs ($|B| \le 4|A|$) run a shuffle-merge
(compare-all-pairs over rotations of both operands --- five permutations
per 8-lane block rather than seven --- with table-driven output
compaction) at cost $c_b\lceil(|A|{+}|B|)/8\rceil$; heavily skewed pairs
run galloping binary search at $\approx c_g|A|\log_2|B|$, admitted only
under the margin test $8\,|A|\log_2|B| < |B|$ so that the branch-heavy
search must dominate the middle tier's linear window advance by a
factor, not merely match it; intermediate skews run a galloping
broadcast scan. Both boundaries were set empirically; replacing the
margin test with a fixed ratio regressed five-way conjunctions by 26\%.

\textbf{Fan-in-aware promotion.} A union key accumulating in array form
re-streams its accumulator at every merge; with fan-in $n$ and summed
cardinality $s$,
\begin{equation}
C_{\mathrm{arr}}(n,s) \approx c_m\,s\Big(\tfrac{n+1}{2}-\tfrac1n\Big),
\qquad
C_{\mathrm{bmp}}(s) \approx c_0 + (c_s{+}c_x)\,s,
\end{equation}
where $c_x$ is the bitmap-to-array conversion of the finished
accumulator, incurred precisely where promotion is contested
($s \le A_{\max}$, so the result re-materializes in array form). The
crossover yields no promotion at $n \le 3$ for any $s \le A_{\max}$ and
thresholds $s \gtrsim 970, 480, 200$ at $n = 4, 5, 8$, encoded as
$\textsc{promote}(n,s) \equiv n \ge 4 \wedge s(n-3) > 1000$. Deployed
Roaring implementations promote at fixed thresholds independent of
fan-in: CRoaring's lazy aggregation defers conversion until an
accumulator exceeds 1024 values, and the container specification
converts at 4096~\cite{chambi2016,lemire2018}. Under the model no fixed
threshold is correct across fan-ins, because the re-streamed volume
grows with $n$; we measured both failure directions: a low fixed
threshold (256) converts the OR-groups of low-fan-in conjunctions into
bitmaps their parent consumes more slowly ($+29\%$ on that shape), and
promoting never strands wide unions in array form ($3.7\times$ on
4-way unions; Table~\ref{tab:abl}). At $n{=}4$ the derived threshold
happens to land near CRoaring's 1024; the rules diverge everywhere else
--- never promoting at $n \le 3$ where a fixed 1024 would, and
promoting at $s \approx 200$ at $n{=}8$ where a fixed 1024 waits
$5\times$ too long. Fan-in bounds come from the weighted shapes of
\S\ref{sec:shapes}.

\textbf{Run containers} fold natively (run$\times$run intersection,
union, and run-splitting difference on typed interval slices) inside
$\B$-clamped slots, converting to bitmaps only on proven overflow.

\section{Fold-Order Selection}
\label{sec:foldorder}

An $n$-ary fold traverses keys $\times$ operands in one of two orders.
\emph{Key-major} order completes each key before the next: one
accumulator remains cache-resident, but each operand stream is resumed
once per key --- $nK$ prefetch restarts over $K$ keys. \emph{Partner-major}
order streams each operand end-to-end against all accumulators:
perfectly sequential input traffic, but the accumulator set is revisited
$n$ times. These are the container-algebra analogues of
document-at-a-time and term-at-a-time postings
traversal~\cite{turtle1995}, and both move identical byte volumes; they
differ only in arrangement relative to the cache hierarchy.

Profiling 16-way differences quantified the key-major penalty: the merge
kernel sustains $1.45\nsu$ per 8-lane block in isolation and $1.56\nsu$
in a single-key control, but $2.17\nsu$ inside a 32-key key-major fold
($+50\%$), attributable to resuming 16 interleaved streams per key.
Partner-major execution recovered the isolated rate --- and dense unions
then exhibited the inverse pathology: with per-key bitmap accumulators
the accumulator set is $K \cdot \B$ (128\,KiB at $K{=}16$), revisiting
it each pass displaces L1, and key-major won by 12\%.

The plan resolves the choice without estimation: the accumulator
footprint $F(P) = \sum_k \capm(k)$ is already proven
(Eq.~\ref{eq:arena}). \fb{} folds partner-major iff
$F(P) \le F_{\max} = 64\,\text{KiB}$ (half of L1D, reserving room for
one streaming operand and the mirror side), in which case the plan
allocates the mirror region and array merges alternate slot sides,
eliminating staging copies. Intersection is the measured exception: a
key-count scaling control (16-way intersection over 1 vs.\ 32 keys:
$695\nsu \to 21.6\us$, i.e.\ $31.1\times$ for $32\times$ the keys) shows
no super-linear term --- the accumulator collapses after the first
operand and subsequent operands are searched, not streamed --- and
partner-major seeding would require clamping by the driver's cardinality
rather than the per-key minimum, weakening
Proposition~\ref{prop:clamp}. Intersection therefore remains key-major
by measurement, difference and union dispatch on $F(P)$.

\section{Liveness Analysis}
\label{sec:shortcircuit}

\subsection{Static container pruning}
\label{sec:holepunch}

\begin{defx}[Liveness mask]
$\mask(T) = K(\mathrm{root}(T))$ under the shape recurrences:
intersection of child key sets at $\wedge$, union at $\vee$, left child
at $\setminus$.
\end{defx}

\begin{lemmax}[Pruning soundness]
\label{lem:mask}
For any leaf $\ell$ and key $k \notin \mask(T)$, deleting container
$c_k(\ell)$ from the input changes no output of $T$. Moreover
$\mask(T)$ over-approximates the true surviving key set, so no
contributing container is pruned.
\end{lemmax}

\begin{proof}[Argument]
Induction on $T$: a key absent from $K(v)$ yields the empty container at
$v$; empty containers are identities for $\vee$, annihilators for
$\wedge$, and left-absorbing for $\setminus$, so absence propagates to
the root. Executor key sets are subsets of shape key sets
(\S\ref{sec:shapes}), giving the over-approximation.
\end{proof}

The planner materializes $\mask(T)$ as an 8\,KiB bitset whenever
$|K(\mathrm{root})| < \max_i |K(v_i)|$ --- exactly the condition under
which pruning is non-vacuous --- and every leaf cursor skips dead keys
in place. The streamed-bytes reduction is
\begin{equation}
\Delta = \sum_{\ell \in \mathrm{leaves}(T)}\;
\sum_{k \in K(\ell)\setminus\mask(T)} \stored\big(c_k(\ell)\big),
\end{equation}
which for the selective-filter benchmark of \S\ref{sec:eval} (one
4-key conjunct against four 256-key disjunction legs) removes 98\% of
input bytes and yields $7.1\us$ versus $241\us$ unpruned; enabling the
automatic derivation improved the full 25k-tree corpus by 15.3\%
(criterion's change-detection test over 10 samples of 20\,s,
$p < 0.05$). Relative to runtime masking for flat
intersections~\cite{roaringjava} and scan-side zone-map
pruning~\cite{moerkotte1998,snowflake2016}, the analysis here is static,
applies to arbitrary operator trees, and carries a soundness argument
tied to the plan's shapes.

\subsection{Compiled short-circuits}

The instruction sequence carries \emph{guards} after each non-flattened
$\wedge$-operand and each $\setminus$-minuend: an empty operand pops the
partial state and branches past the fold, so sibling subtrees are never
evaluated. Guards use relative offsets and therefore survive splicing
into enclosing plans. Within operators, intersection fuses population
count into the word-level AND and abandons a key on emptiness; per-key
folds order operands most-selective-first; partner-major passes skip
dead slots. A filter containing a contradictory subexpression evaluates
in $143\nsu$ against $738\us$ for the better baseline build, since
the expensive sibling is never executed; we report this as a property
of guard
placement rather than a headline result, and note the baseline's
operator API offers no way to express sibling short-circuiting, so the
row compares expressiveness rather than kernels.

\section{Memory Discipline}
\label{sec:memory}

Four per-thread pools back execution: op arenas (Eq.~\ref{eq:arena}),
fold cursor/reference scratch, the evaluator's operand stack, and result
buffers. Pooled buffers are stored empty and loaned at the caller's
lifetime; intermediate arenas chain directly as operator inputs, and
in-place serialization (Proposition~\ref{prop:inplace}) swaps the
arena's buffer out as the result while a pooled result buffer rotates
back. Steady-state evaluation therefore performs zero heap allocations
--- verified by sampling profiles in which the allocator does not
appear --- and first use on a thread allocates once, after which
buffers circulate. The comparable baseline cost is visible in its own
profiles: under the library's pairwise operators our workloads
attribute roughly 6\% of runtime to the allocator (its \texttt{MultiOps}
aggregation path allocates less, via copy-on-write container borrowing,
but is not allocation-free), before any tail-latency effects.

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

\textbf{Setup.} Apple M4 Pro (aarch64/NEON); rustc 1.96.0 for \fb{} and
the default baseline, rustc 1.98.0-nightly for the baseline's
\texttt{simd} feature; criterion medians over 30 samples $\times$ 4\,s
per benchmark (corpus: 10 $\times$ 20\,s), typical confidence
half-widths under 2\%. Baselines: Rust \texttt{roaring} 0.11.4 in both
configurations --- the default build (scalar array kernels; what
stable-toolchain users run) and the nightly-only \texttt{simd} build ---
interleaved by a two-pass runner into one report, then a dedicated
third pass re-measures the \fb{} cells alone under the same nightly
build. The third pass exists because sub-microsecond \fb{} cells drift
up to $+70\%$ inside the 20-minute interleaved passes --- ambient
allocator pressure from the alloc-heavy baseline benchmarks, which no
short-context measurement reproduces --- while baseline cells, whose
own allocation traffic dominates that noise floor, show no such drift
and keep their interleaved numbers. All $n$-ary baseline
calls go through the library's \texttt{MultiOps} trait, its documented
fast path for merging many bitmaps (lazy copy-on-write promotion,
largest-first ordering); binary nodes use the pairwise operators. All
\fb{} tree measurements include plan construction.
Correctness: differential suites against Roaring (random trees, pruned
trees, run containers, 10M-element round trips) and the 11.6k-case
clamp stress; every configuration reported here passes all suites.

\subsection{Operation sweep}

Table~\ref{tab:sweep} reports the full 2--16-way sweep over three
container regimes; all 36 cells of all three systems come from one
run of the protocol above. \fb{} wins 34 cells against both builds:
every sorted-array cell ($1.25$--$12.2\times$ over default,
$1.26$--$2.9\times$ over SIMD), every packed-bitmap cell
($1.05$--$3.8\times$) except a binary-difference tie
($0.97$--$0.98\times$), and every run-container cell
($1.01$--$1.69\times$) except 16-way intersection ($0.87\times$), the
sweep's single loss.

The run-container regime did not start this way, and its repair is a
case study in the cost model of \S\ref{sec:intro}. Run cells carry
$\sim$18-byte payloads, so an earlier version lost the binary cells
outright ($0.68\times$ at binary run difference): decomposition showed
the planning walk alone was 282\,ns of the 781\,ns total, the fold
including arena setup was at parity with the baseline's entire
operation, and in-place serialization measured at noise --- the cells
isolated the price of the analysis pass, with no data volume to
amortize it against. Three measured responses (ablation rows in
Table~\ref{tab:abl}) recovered the regime: pooling the plan's
buffers, a hybrid seed-driven plan walk with an inlined cursor fast
path, and --- decisively --- capacity-analysis-free plans for tiny
$\wedge$/$\setminus$ one-shots, which clamp every slot at $\B$ (sound:
every fold state fits a bitmap slot, and the driving input's key set
is a superset whose unclaimed slots serialize to nothing). The
remaining loss is structural rather than analytical: 16-way run
intersection folds $n{-}1$ sequential native-run passes where the
baseline aggregates; a $k$-way run merge is concrete future work.

\subsection{Ablations}
\label{sec:eval:abl}

Every mechanism was admitted under a keep-only-if-faster rule;

\subsection{Kernel parity}

Because the claims concern the planning layer, we verified the kernels
are not the explanation: per-block throughput against the baseline's
SIMD kernels at equal work sits within a $0$ to $+16\%$ band. A kernel
edge of that size cannot account for the multiples in
Tables~\ref{tab:sweep}--\ref{tab:trees}, but it is not zero; the
sharper attribution experiment --- the baseline's kernels running under
\fb's plans --- is future work, and until then the end-to-end
differences are attributable primarily, not exclusively, to planning,
pruning, and memory discipline.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Four limitations bound the present evaluation. (1)~All measurements are
from one aarch64 microarchitecture; the x86 (SSE) kernel ports compile
and are algorithmically identical, but their performance and the
transfer of Table~\ref{tab:constants}'s constants are unvalidated.
(2)~The baseline family is the Rust Roaring library; CRoaring ships
per-pair dispatch heuristics its Rust port lacks, so a CRoaring
comparison with per-mechanism attribution is required before the
end-to-end multiples can be read as pure planning-layer gains.
(3)~Operands and trees are synthetic (deterministic generators);
standard Roaring real-data corpora and production filter traces remain
future work. (4)~Interior-node bounds are sound but loose, and static
dispatch on loose bounds forgoes the adaptivity of dynamic
schemes~\cite{raman2007}; quantifying bound tightness and
mis-dispatch frequency against an oracle is open. The evaluation is
single-threaded; the per-thread pool design has not been characterized
under concurrency.

\section{Deployment}
\label{sec:deployment}

\fb{} is deployed as the filtering substrate of Exa, a commercial
web-scale search engine, where every boolean filter predicate compiles
to a \fb{} expression evaluated against frozen per-segment bitmaps
served from memory maps. Plans are per-(expression, segment) --- the
analysis runs against each segment's exact leaf metadata, which is what
makes its guarantees possible, and its cost sits on the query path
exactly as in every measurement of \S\ref{sec:eval}. The deployment
exercises the design as intended --- immutable segments produced by an
indexing pipeline, allocation-free steady-state evaluation --- and
motivated the original design constraints, in particular the removal
of allocator traffic from the tail. A quantitative production
characterization (tail-latency distributions, filter-shape statistics,
fleet-scale resource accounting) is beyond the scope of this paper.

\section{Conclusion}

For boolean filtering over compressed bitmaps in the frozen-segment
serving regime, the optimization surface that prior work resolves
dynamically can be discharged entirely at plan time, and doing so yields
guarantees dynamic schemes cannot offer: allocation-free execution under
a closed-form memory bound, sound static pruning at container
granularity across arbitrary expression trees, and fold orders selected
from proven footprints. On kernels measured at parity with the
baseline's, these plan-time guarantees account for the bulk of wins in
34 of 36 sweep cells --- sorted-array operations by
$1.25$--$12.2\times$ --- and $2.2$--$2.7\times$ end-to-end against the
standard Rust Roaring library's documented fast paths, with the single
remaining run-container loss (\S\ref{sec:eval}) bounding where \fb's
static policies currently pay.
The system, its differential
and stress suites, the 25k-tree corpus, and the audit tooling are open
source.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/WilliXL/frostbit}}
